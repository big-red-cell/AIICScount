### The problem


#### Please describe the technical details of the vulnerability

Description:
The issue arises when URLs containing encoded backslashes and email-style domain specifications lead to unexpected redirects. For instance, entering google.com@yahoo.com in the browser address bar redirects users to yahoo.com instead of resolving as an invalid URL or parsing google.com as the primary domain. Similarly, URLs encoded with backslashes (e.g., https://example.com%5C%5C@google.com/) redirect incorrectly, depending on browser interpretation, which could potentially lead to phishing attacks or other security vulnerabilities as users might end up on unintended websites.

Impact:
This behavior can mislead users about the destination of a URL, increasing the risk of phishing and other malicious activities where the apparent domain in the URL does not match the destination. The impact is significant as it affects user trust and data security, providing a vector through which attackers could craft URLs that deceive users about their true destination, possibly leading to unauthorized information disclosure or loss.

Any unauthenticated user
