<h3>The problem</h3>
<h4>Please describe the technical details of the vulnerability</h4>
<p>Vulnerability Summary: The vulnerability exists in Google Chrome&#39;s password manager, where saved passwords are exposed in clear text, even after the user logs out of their Google account. If the user has previously signed in to their Google account and remains registered in the browser, the passwords remain accessible through Chrome&#39;s password manager interface without requiring re-authentication or any form of additional security check.</p>
<p>Steps to Reproduce:</p>
<ol>
<li>Log in to a Google account with saved passwords in Google Chrome</li>
<li>Log out of the Google account within the browser (ensuring that the user is no longer logged in).</li>
<li>Go to the Chrome password manager (chrome://password-manager/passwords).
The passwords are still visible in clear text without any prompt for authentication, even though the Google account is logged out.
Impact: This vulnerability exposes saved passwords in clear text, allowing any user with physical access to the device to view sensitive credentials without requiring further authentication or access to the Google account. This could lead to unauthorized access to personal or sensitive accounts, data breaches, and other forms of exploitation.</li>
</ol>
<p>Possible Mitigation:</p>
<ol>
<li>Implement an additional layer of authentication (e.g., password or biometric authentication) before showing saved passwords in the password manager, even after the user logs out of their Google account.</li>
<li>Ensure that Chrome&#39;s password manager requires re-authentication or an additional security check to access saved passwords when the account is logged out.</li>
</ol>
<h4>Please briefly explain who can exploit the vulnerability, and what they gain when doing so</h4>
<p>Who can exploit the vulnerability?
Anyone with physical access to the device where the Google Chrome browser is installed and has saved passwords, even if the user is logged out of their Google account. This includes not only malicious individuals but also members of institutions with shared computers, such as schools or workplaces, where multiple users have access to the same device.</p>
<p>What do they gain?
By exploiting this vulnerability, an attacker can view saved passwords stored in Chrome’s password manager in clear text. This gives them access to any accounts, websites, or services that the user has saved credentials for. In shared environments, this could lead to identity theft, unauthorized access to sensitive accounts, theft of bank information, and potential data breaches affecting multiple users.</p>

<h3>The cause</h3>
<h4>What version of Chrome have you found the security issue in?</h4>
<p>The vulnerability appears to be a persistent issue and has likely been present in multiple versions of Google Chrome for some time, as it is related to the behavior of the password manager, which has not changed significantly in recent updates. It was observed in Google Chrome version 131.0.6778.265, but it is likely to affect older versions as well.</p>
