<p><strong>VULNERABILITY DETAILS</strong></p>
<p>getDisplayMedia() appears to allow sites to repeatedly spam users with modal dialogs until they give in to potentially unsafe screen-sharing.</p>
<p><strong>VERSION</strong></p>
<p>Chrome Version       : 89.0.4389.114 (Official Build) (x86_64)<br>
Operating System: macOS 10.15.7 (but seems irrelevant)<br>
URLs (if applicable) : <a href="https://jsfiddle.net/jib1/q75yb8pf/77" data-b-widget="url" data-b-original-href="https://jsfiddle.net/jib1/q75yb8pf/77" data-b-redirected-href="https://www.google.com/url?q=https://jsfiddle.net/jib1/q75yb8pf/77&amp;sa=D&amp;source=buganizer&amp;usg=AOvVaw062hLATd5Bjy06ysURGCCL">https://jsfiddle.net/jib1/q75yb8pf/77</a><br>
Other browsers tested:<br>
Safari: OK<br>
Firefox: OK<br>
Edge: FAIL</p>
<p><strong>REPRODUCTION CASE</strong></p>
<p>(1) Open <a href="https://jsfiddle.net/jib1/q75yb8pf/77" data-b-widget="url" data-b-original-href="https://jsfiddle.net/jib1/q75yb8pf/77" data-b-redirected-href="https://www.google.com/url?q=https://jsfiddle.net/jib1/q75yb8pf/77&amp;sa=D&amp;source=buganizer&amp;usg=AOvVaw062hLATd5Bjy06ysURGCCL">https://jsfiddle.net/jib1/q75yb8pf/77</a><br>
(2) Click &quot;Cancel&quot; in the modal screen picker that appears.</p>
<p>What is the expected result?</p>
<ul>
<li>The dialog goes away and doesn&#39;t come back.</li>
</ul>
<p>What happens instead?</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>The dialog comes back immediately, and won&#39;t go away unless I do one of two things:</p>
<ol>
<li>Select something (full screen being the presented and most obvious choice) and click &quot;Share&quot;,</li>
<li>Close the tab.</li>
</ol>
</li>
</ul>
<p>A malicious site may hold naive-to-moderately technical users hostage and increase conversion to allowing the site access to their full screen, which is opens users to security and privacy attacks. See <a href="https://blog.mozilla.org/webrtc/share-browser-windows-entire-screen-sites-trust/" data-b-widget="url" data-b-original-href="https://blog.mozilla.org/webrtc/share-browser-windows-entire-screen-sites-trust/" data-b-redirected-href="https://www.google.com/url?q=https://blog.mozilla.org/webrtc/share-browser-windows-entire-screen-sites-trust/&amp;sa=D&amp;source=buganizer&amp;usg=AOvVaw3L9mzztKcHM7mqhpZYs0_D">https://blog.mozilla.org/webrtc/share-browser-windows-entire-screen-sites-trust/</a></p>
<p>While users may easily close the tab, this avenue isn&#39;t super-obvious given the modal UX appearance, and takes some experimentation to figure out.</p>
<p>Other browsers avoid this in two ways:</p>
<ol>
<li>They implement transient activation, as required by the spec. <a href="https://crbug.com/1198918" data-b-widget="url" data-b-original-href="https://crbug.com/1198918" data-b-redirected-href="https://www.google.com/url?q=https://crbug.com/1198918&amp;sa=D&amp;source=buganizer&amp;usg=AOvVaw17KE2Q1eeR40Ca_9LC9fh7">https://crbug.com/1198918</a></li>
<li>They implement a temporary block after the first NotAllowedError, as allowed by the spec (e.g. Firefox).</li>
</ol>
<p>The former would reduce the issue to when the user clicks the page again. That latter would help prompt-spamming on further interaction with the site, a protection common with similar permission-based APIs like getUserMedia().</p>

Author: el...@chromium.org
I urge caution when applying throttling to media-capture. The case is not as straight-forward as with e.g. geolocation.<br>Consider a VC application. The hypothetical user (X) clicks &quot;share&quot; and |getDisplayMedia| is called, but while the user is sifting through the media-picker dialog, our participant&#39;s manager (Y) changes the topic, so X dismisses the dialog and resumes listening. Twenty seconds later Y encourages X to speak again, at which point X clicks &quot;share&quot; once more. If throttling happens, the X will have to reload the page before they can share, and this could be quite annoying and embarrassing. I am familiar with one browser that throttles after the first call. I argue that this would be problematic even after the N-th call. Users toying with the media-picker might not realize that they have N times to do so before the browser decides that they&#39;re being harassed. IMHO, best not throttle, at least not without a lengthier discussion. I think user activation is enough.
