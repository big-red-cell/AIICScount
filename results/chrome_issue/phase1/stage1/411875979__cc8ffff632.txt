<h3>The problem</h3>
<h4>Please describe the technical details of the vulnerability</h4>
<p>The domain <code>https://www.google.com.google.evil.com/</code> is fully controlled by an attacker (<code>evil.com</code>), but it is intentionally crafted to appear as if it belongs to <code>google.com</code>. This is a case of domain-based spoofing or visual domain confusion.</p>
<p>There is no vulnerable redirect parameter on the target&#39;s server — instead, the trust is abused by exploiting how subdomains are displayed in many user interfaces.</p>
<p>Most users will only see part of the URL (e.g., <code>https://www.google.com...</code>), especially on mobile devices, chat apps, or email clients, and assume it&#39;s a legitimate Google domain.</p>
<h3>Example:</h3>
<p><a href="https://www.google.com.google.evil.com/" data-b-widget="url" data-b-original-href="https://www.google.com.google.evil.com/" data-b-redirected-href="https://www.google.com/url?q=https://www.google.com.google.evil.com/&amp;sa=D&amp;source=buganizer&amp;usg=AOvVaw0G_Nig2bVqnWRhYWp6KZDF">https://www.google.com.google.evil.com/</a></p>
<h4>Impact analysis – Please briefly explain who can exploit the vulnerability, and what they gain when doing so</h4>
<h3>Impact</h3>
<p>This type of spoofed domain can be used in phishing campaigns to:</p>
<ul>
<li>Trick users into entering credentials on a fake login page</li>
<li>Steal OAuth tokens via social engineering</li>
<li>Bypass email filters that allow trusted domains (e.g., <code>google.com</code>)</li>
<li>Deliver malicious content under the appearance of a safe link</li>
</ul>
<p>This can lead to credential theft, account compromise, and brand abuse, ultimately affecting user trust and security.</p>
