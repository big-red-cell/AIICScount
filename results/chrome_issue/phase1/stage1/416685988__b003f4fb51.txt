<p>Interestingly, I get the same result whether the PoC is hosted at bugs.szymsza.cz or it is opened directly as a local file, suggesting that the site of a local file is not determined properly. In that case, an attacker could infer the availability of an arbitrary resource, as long as they trick the user into downloading and opening a file.</p>
<h1>Problem Description</h1>
<p>When embedding a cross-origin same-site non-video resource into a <span>&lt;video&gt;</span> element, the MediaError message can leak the status of the resource, since it is different for existing and non-existing resources.</p>
<p>This same-origin policy violation can be dangerous, since it can lead to cross-origin leaks, such as inferring the login status of the user. Although it is slightly less severe than cross-site leaks, it can still lead to undesired consequences in case subdomains are controlled by different organizations, such as various .github.io pages.</p>
<p>This is similar to the bug <a href="https://issues.chromium.org/issues/40090985" data-b-widget="issue-reference" data-b-issue-id="40090985">https://issues.chromium.org/issues/40090985</a> but concerns cross-origin same-site URLs, not cross-site cross-origin.</p>
<p>Additionally, the Firefox bug <a href="https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1731614" data-b-widget="url" data-b-original-href="https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1731614" data-b-redirected-href="https://www.google.com/url?q=https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id%3D1731614&amp;sa=D&amp;source=buganizer&amp;usg=AOvVaw2Zewd7v5W0DqhvbWbcMR0_">https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1731614</a> is very similar, but in their case, the leak could only occur if the URL port differed, and not if the hostname differed.</p>
<p>The bug seems to affect versions 71-74 and then from 79 until the latest version.</p>
