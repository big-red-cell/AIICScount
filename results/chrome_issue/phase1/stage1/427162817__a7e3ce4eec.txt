<h3>The problem</h3>
<h4>Please describe the technical details of the vulnerability</h4>
<p>This issue occurs when a user adds a dot after &quot;youtube.com&quot; in the URL (i.e., youtube.com.). While this still loads the video correctly, it prevents YouTube from showing any ads.</p>
<p>Technically, this works because the browser treats youtube.com. as a different domain from youtube.com. As a result:</p>
<p>Cookies set for youtube.com — especially those related to ads and tracking — do not apply to youtube.com..</p>
<p>Without these cookies, YouTube&#39;s ad system fails to load or display ads.</p>
<p>No browser extensions or developer tools are needed — it&#39;s a simple manual change to the URL.</p>
<p>Because the ads are skipped completely, this creates a potential way for users to bypass YouTube’s monetization, which can harm content creators and the platform’s revenue.</p>
<h4>Impact analysis – Please briefly explain who can exploit the vulnerability, and what they gain when doing so</h4>
<p>By adding a dot (.) after youtube.com in any video URL (e.g., <a href="https://www.youtube.com./watch?v=VIDEO_ID" data-b-widget="url" data-b-original-href="https://www.youtube.com./watch?v=VIDEO_ID" data-b-redirected-href="https://www.google.com/url?q=https://www.youtube.com./watch?v%3DVIDEO_ID&amp;sa=D&amp;source=buganizer&amp;usg=AOvVaw3jBZ3qXDS3d3JHFJmZNdTM">https://www.youtube.com./watch?v=VIDEO_ID</a>), users are able to completely bypass all forms of ads — including pre-roll, mid-roll, banner, and overlay ads — without using Premium or ad blockers.</p>
<p>Potential Impact:</p>
<ol>
<li>Monetization Loss for Creators:</li>
</ol>
<p>Ads are a key source of income for millions of YouTube creators.</p>
<p>This loophole allows viewers to watch content without supporting the creators financially.</p>
<p>If the trick spreads, creators could see a noticeable drop in ad revenue despite having high view counts.</p>
<ol start="2">
<li>Exploitation at Scale:</li>
</ol>
<p>The trick is extremely simple to replicate — just one dot in the URL.</p>
<p>It can be automated using scripts, browser extensions, or even shared as &quot;hacks&quot; in viral content.</p>
<p>If not patched, millions of views could bypass ads, leading to significant revenue loss.</p>
<ol start="3">
<li>Weakens YouTube&#39;s Ad Ecosystem:</li>
</ol>
<p>Advertisers pay to reach viewers via YouTube’s trusted ad delivery system.</p>
<p>If users can easily bypass ads, advertiser trust may decline, impacting the platform’s reputation and ad revenue.</p>
<ol start="4">
<li>Creates an Unfair User Experience:</li>
</ol>
<p>Honest users watch ads to support creators.</p>
<p>This exploit creates a free-rider loophole where some users benefit from ad-free content without paying (no Premium, no support).</p>
<ol start="5">
<li>Long-Term Risk:</li>
</ol>
<p>If the trick becomes widely shared, it could impact:</p>
<p>YouTube’s CPM performance</p>
<p>Ad bidding and targeting accuracy</p>
<p>Perceived value of ad space on YouTube</p>

Works on Chrome and Edge</p>
