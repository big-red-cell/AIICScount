<p>Thank you for the report.</p>
<p>I understand the concern you are raising, but this is working as designed. Passwords are difficult to keep secure from someone who is accessing your computer logged on to your account, for reasons described in <a href="https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/HEAD/docs/security/faq.md#if-there_s-a-way-to-see-stored-passwords-without-entering-a-password_is-this-a-security-bug" data-b-widget="url" data-b-original-href="https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/HEAD/docs/security/faq.md#if-there_s-a-way-to-see-stored-passwords-without-entering-a-password_is-this-a-security-bug" data-b-redirected-href="https://www.google.com/url?q=https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/%2B/HEAD/docs/security/faq.md%23if-there_s-a-way-to-see-stored-passwords-without-entering-a-password_is-this-a-security-bug&amp;sa=D&amp;source=buganizer&amp;usg=AOvVaw1zbKuEUoocSyxdJVZ6VGq_">the Chrome Security FAQ</a>. The &quot;hiding&quot; of passwords in password fields where we replace the text with dots is really just a protection against accidental exposure to other people who might be looking at your screen. You&#39;re right that there is additional risk of that with the autofill option to fill a password in any field, but we try to mitigate that by UI that requires several clicks to get to, so it is unlikely to be done by accident.</p>
<p>Also, if you have Windows Hello enabled on your computer then there will be an option in chrome://password-manager/settings to require a user verification before <em>any</em> autofill of a password. This would mean entering the PIN or providing a biometric that you have registered with Windows Hello.</p>
