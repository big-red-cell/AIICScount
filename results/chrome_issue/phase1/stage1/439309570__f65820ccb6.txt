<h1>Steps to reproduce the problem</h1>
<p>Hi Team,</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>In <strong>Chrome Version 139.0.7258.128 on macOS Sequoia 15.6</strong>, the <strong>Bluetooth</strong> and <strong>USB chooser dialogs</strong> do not display any origin information when the initiator origin is <strong>opaque</strong>.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>This occurs because the dialogs rely on <code>LastCommittedOrigin()</code> for the initiating frame, and when that origin is opaque, the UI shows an <strong>empty string</strong>. As a result, a malicious site can embed an opaque iframe to suppress origin display and trick users into granting sensitive device permissions without transparency.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>This issue reduces the security value of chooser dialogs and creates a risk of <strong>origin spoofing and deceptive consent</strong>.</p>
</li>
</ul>
<h3>Proof of concept:</h3>
<ul>
<li>I have uploaded the proof of concept video in this report for your review and reference.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Affected Versions</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Chrome:</strong> 139.0.7258.128</li>
<li><strong>OS:</strong> macOS Sequoia 15.6</li>
</ul>

<strong>Expected Behavior:</strong> Chooser dialog should show the <strong>top-level page’s origin</strong>, or block the dialog entirely when initiator origin is opaque.</p>
<h1>Problem Description</h1>
<h3>Impact:</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Origin Transparency Lost:</strong> Users cannot see which site is requesting device access.</li>
<li><strong>Spoofing &amp; Phishing Risk:</strong> Attackers can embed opaque iframes to hide their identity during sensitive Bluetooth/USB permission requests.</li>
<li><strong>Practical Exploitation:</strong> A malicious site could trick users into granting device access (e.g., microphones, keyboards, or Bluetooth peripherals) without origin attribution, leading to potential data exfiltration or unauthorized interactions.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Recommendation:</h3>
<ul>
<li>Ensure chooser dialogs <strong>never render with a blank origin</strong>.</li>
<li>If initiator is opaque, fallback to <strong>top-level origin</strong> or prevent the chooser from appearing.</li>
<li>Add defensive checks to handle opaque origins safely across all platforms (Android, macOS, Windows).</li>
</ul>
