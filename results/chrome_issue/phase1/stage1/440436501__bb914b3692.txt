Author: # Steps to reproduce the problem
Here’s the most common attack vector:

1.  **Session Hijacking:** The attacker gained access to your active browser session. This could happen through:
    *   **XSS (Cross-Site Scripting)** on a website you visited that allowed them to steal your session cookie.
    *   **Malicious Extension** that harvested your cookies or performed actions on your behalf.
    *   **Physical Access** to your unlocked computer.
    *   **Man-in-the-Middle Attack** on an unsecured network (though much harder with HTTPS prevalent).

2.  **Initiation of Family Link:** With your active session, the attacker navigates to the Google Family Link page (`families.google.com`). Since they are "you," they can create a new family group.

3.  **Making Themselves the Parent:** The attacker adds their *own* Google account as the "parent" or "supervisor" in the family group.

4.  **Adding You as the Child:** They then add *your* Google account as the "child" in this group. Google sends a notification to your account, but it might be easily dismissed or mistaken for spam.

5.  **Accepting the Request (The Crucial Part):** The attacker, still in your active session, can immediately accept the parental control request *on your behalf*. This completes the link. Their account is now the supervisor of your account.

**Why this is so powerful:** Once the link is established, the "parent" account (the attacker) can control:
*   **Screen Time:** Set daily time limits for your entire device or specific apps.
*   **Content Filters:** Block "adult" websites or only allow pre-approved sites.
*   **App Restrictions:** Approve or block any app downloads from the Play Store.
*   **Location Tracking:** See your device's location in near-real-time.
*   **Account Supervision:** Enforce SafeSearch, etc.
*   **Remote Lock:** Lock your device.

### Recreating it for Your Bug Bounty Report

**Required Setup for Ethical Recreation:**
*   **Two test Google accounts:** `attacker@example.com` and `victim@example.com`
*   **A clean browser or incognito window.**
*   **A detailed screen recording tool** (like OBS) to capture every step.

**Step-by-Step Recreation Steps (for your report):**

1.  **Precondition:** Log in to the `victim@example.com` account in your browser. This simulates the attacker having hijacked the user's session.

2.  **Navigate to Family Link:** While logged in as the "victim," go to `families.google.com`.

3.  **Create Family Group:** Click "Create a family group." The victim's account is initiating the process.

4.  **Invite "Parent":** You will be asked to invite family members. Invite your other test account (`attacker@example.com`) and assign it the role of **"Parent"**.
    *   *This is the core of the vulnerability: a user should not be able to assign a parent to their own account without explicit, verified consent from the parent-to-be account.*

5.  **Switch Context (Simulate Attack):** Open a new incognito window or different browser. Log in to the `attacker@example.com` account. Check the email for the invitation and accept it. The attacker is now part of the family group as a parent.

6.  **Complete the Hijack:** Go back to the browser window where you are still logged in as the `victim`. The victim account now needs to "finish setting up" the family group by adding themselves as a child.
    *   The interface will guide the victim to download Family Link *on their device*. **BUT** the critical flaw is that the "parent" (attacker) can often initiate this from their management console, or the victim can be tricked into completing this step themselves during the initial session hijack.

7.  **Demonstrate Impact:** Show that the `attacker@example.com` account can now log in to the Family Link parent portal and control the settings of the `victim@example.com` account.
    *   Show the dashboard.
    *   Demonstrate changing a setting (e.g., setting a bedtime for the victim).
    *   Explain the full scope of control (list the points from above).
