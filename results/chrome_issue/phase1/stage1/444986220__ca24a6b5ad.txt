<h3>The problem</h3>
<h4>Please describe the technical details of the vulnerability</h4>
<p>It is possible to spoof the address bar by dragging and dropping a crafted URL into the omnibox.
During navigation, the omnibox displays the dropped URL (attacker-controlled domain) while the old page’s scripts/content are still active. This leads to a window where the address bar misrepresents the actual origin, enabling phishing or UI/URL spoofing.</p>

<h4>Impact analysis – Please briefly explain who can exploit the vulnerability, and what they gain when doing so</h4>
<p>If exploited, this vulnerability allows an attacker to make the browser’s address bar display a fake, trusted-looking domain while the page content remains under attacker control. As a result, users can be tricked into entering credentials, revealing sensitive data, or authorizing dangerous actions under the false belief they are interacting with a legitimate site. This significantly facilitates phishing and social-engineering attacks especially dangerous if the technique can be triggered with minimal user interaction because many users do not reliably verify security indicators such as the URL or lock icon before entering sensitive information.</p>

<p>This behaviour is deliberate, because we consider a URL that was dragged to the omnibox to have been entered by the user.
