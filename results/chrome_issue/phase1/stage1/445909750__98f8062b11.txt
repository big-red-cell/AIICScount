<h3>The problem</h3>
<h4>Please describe the technical details of the vulnerability</h4>
<p>When a user signs out of Chrome, saved credentials are still accessible via Settings → Autofill → Passwords.
Only the Windows account password is required to reveal credentials, not the Google account password.
This allows anyone with local access to the machine to view all stored usernames and passwords.
The logout option gives users a false sense of security as Chrome does not fully protect the password store.
On shared or public computers, this greatly increases the risk of credential theft.
Impact: Unauthorized disclosure of all saved accounts despite Chrome sign-out.
Root Cause: Chrome relies solely on OS authentication instead of requiring Google re-authentication.
Expected Behavior: Google account password or Chrome-specific PIN/biometric should be enforced post-logout.
Fix Recommendation: Require Google re-authentication (with 2FA if enabled) or a Chrome-level lock for password viewing.
Severity: High/Critical – exposure of all saved credentials to unauthorized users.</p>
<h4>Impact analysis – Please briefly explain who can exploit the vulnerability, and what they gain when doing so</h4>
<p>Who can exploit it: anyone with local or remote access to the target machine who can unlock the OS (e.g., a person with the Windows password, an insider, someone with RDP/remote session or malware running as the user), or an attacker who convinces the user to perform the verification on a compromised/ public device.</p>
<p>Prerequisites: ability to unlock the OS account (enter the Windows password) or already having an active session on the device.</p>
<p>What the attacker gains: full disclosure of all credentials saved in Chrome’s password manager (usernames, passwords, and potentially associated metadata).</p>
<p>Consequences: account takeover of personal and corporate services, unauthorized access to email/finance/social accounts, privilege escalation, data exfiltration, and potential lateral movement inside corporate networks.</p>
<p>Collateral risks: credential reuse amplifies impact (compromise of other services), and harvested credentials can be sold or used for targeted phishing.</p>
<p>Severity rationale: disclosure of all saved passwords constitutes a high-to-critical impact due to immediate, broad, and lasting account compromise.</p>

Author: Hi Chromium Security Team,

Thank you for the clarification and for pointing me to the FAQ. I
understand that this behavior is considered "intended" since Chrome relies
on the operating system's authentication model for local credential
protection.

That said, I would like to highlight a potential *security design concern*
from a user perspective:

   -

   Users typically assume that *signing out of Chrome* will also protect
   their stored credentials from being accessed.
   -

   Currently, even after signing out, Chrome can still display or autofill
   stored passwords if someone can authenticate at the OS level.
   -

   This creates a security/UX gap in scenarios where a device is shared
   (e.g., family computers, workplace machines) or when a user signs out of
   Chrome but leaves the Windows session unlocked.

Other browsers and password managers often mitigate this by requiring a
secondary factor (PIN, master password, biometric) before showing or
autofilling credentials. Introducing a similar mechanism in Chrome could
reduce the risk of unintended credential disclosure and better align with
user expectations around "sign out" meaning "secure."

I understand this may not qualify as a security vulnerability under
Chrome's current threat model, but I hope you'll consider this as a *security
hardening / defense-in-depth suggestion*.

Thank you for your time and for clarifying Chrome's stance on this.

Best regards,
Hassan Nadeem
