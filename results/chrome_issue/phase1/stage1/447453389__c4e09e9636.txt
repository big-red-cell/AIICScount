<h1>Steps to reproduce the problem</h1>
<ul>
<li>Visit <code>https://profound-piroshki-a16724.netlify.app/protocolspoofing.html</code> — a page that triggers external protocol handlers from a different origin.</li>
<li>The external protocol handlers we are testing (tel, sms, mail) are hosted at:
<code>https://attacker.hackerbro.net/tel.php</code>, <code>https://attacker.hackerbro.net/sms.php</code>, and <code>https://attacker.hackerbro.net/mail.php</code>.</li>
<li>On <code>https://profound-piroshki-a16724.netlify.app/protocolspoofing.html</code>, click the “Initiate tel”, “Initiate sms”, and “Initiate mail” protocol-handler buttons one by one.</li>
<li>Observe that the external protocol content does not show its origin and is rendered cross-origin within the current page. That is the issue.</li>
</ul>
<h1>Problem Description</h1>
<ul>
<li>During my research on <strong>Chrome Android</strong>, I identified an issue where <strong>external protocol handlers</strong> can be spoofed through a combination of <strong>server-side redirects</strong> and <strong>cross-origin rendering</strong>.</li>
<li>A URL that responds with a <strong>server-side redirect</strong> to an external protocol can trigger the external protocol handler.</li>
<li>On <strong>Chrome Android</strong>, no origin is displayed. Server-side redirects combined with cross-origin rendering can open external protocols, creating a risk of origin confusion and potential phishing or malicious app invocation.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Test Environment:</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Browser:</strong> Chrome 140.0.7339.207 (Stable), Chrome Beta 141.0.7390.27</li>
<li><strong>OS:</strong> Android</li>
</ul>
<h3>Impact:</h3>
<ul>
<li>Users may be misled into believing the external protocol request originated from a trusted site.</li>
<li>This allows attackers to <strong>spoof origins</strong> and trick users into opening unsafe apps or URLs.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Recommended Fix:</h3>
<ul>
<li>The browser should <strong>not allow cross-origin rendering</strong> to suppress or replace the true origin.</li>
<li>The <strong>true initiating origin of the external protocol request</strong> must be displayed clearly in the dialogue prompt before launching the external protocol.</li>
<li>Alternatively, block or sanitize external protocol handler invocations triggered via server-side redirects.</li>
</ul>
