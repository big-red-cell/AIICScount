<h3>The problem</h3>
<h4>Please describe the technical details of the vulnerability</h4>
<p>After logging into the victim’s Google account, a passkey was registered from the attacker’s device.
When the account password was changed (presumably by the user), the attacker lost access via password—but retained access via the registered passkey.
No re-authentication or approval was required from the new session.</p>
<h4>Impact analysis – Please briefly explain who can exploit the vulnerability, and what they gain when doing so</h4>
<p>Anyone who previously gained temporary access to the Google account (e.g., via phishing, credential reuse, or malware).

<p>Hello, thanks for the report. This is working as intended. Passkeys are designed to work independently of passwords. In the scenario you describe, the attacker can already do a lot of damage if they got access to the victim&#39;s google account. If however, the user realized their account was compromised and took the remedial action of changing their password, they would also have the opportunity to review all the registered passkeys and remove any they didn&#39;t recognize.</p>
