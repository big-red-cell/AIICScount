<h1>Problem Description</h1>
<p>The webpage is designed so that users must click several times before the credit card fields become active. An attacker can take advantage of this by hiding or overlaying a malicious element in the same area, causing the user to unknowingly click on it. These unintended clicks can trigger the automatic submission of the user’s stored credit card information. As a result, sensitive data may be sent without the user realizing it, creating a risk of unauthorized credit card data exposure.</p>

<p>Root Cause (Simplified Explanation)</p>
<ol>
<li>The user thinks they are tapping the normal Continue button.</li>
<li>But the tap is actually being captured by a hidden, zero-opacity input field that is placed on top of the button.</li>
<li>When this invisible field receives the tap, it becomes focused.</li>
<li>Focusing the field triggers the credit-card bottom sheet to open.</li>
<li>The bottom sheet shows another Continue button in the exact same screen position, so the user’s next tap accidentally activates it — without realizing it.</li>
</ol>
