<h1>Steps to reproduce the problem</h1>
<ol>
<li>Open Google Chrome Canary (145.0.7615.0 (Official Build), 64-bit) supporting Tab Groups.</li>
<li>Create a new Tab Group and open <strong>Tab A</strong> within the group.</li>
<li>Load a page in Tab A containing a payload that requests multiple permissions (e.g., Geolocation, Camera, Microphone, Notifications, Clipboard, Bluetooth, Persistent Storage).</li>
<li>Open <strong>Tab B</strong> and <strong>Tab C</strong> in the same Tab Group (they may be empty or load different origins).</li>
<li>Open <strong>Tab D</strong> outside the group (any domain).</li>
<li>Execute or trigger the payload in <strong>Tab A</strong> (via user gesture or automatic script) that requests permission.</li>
<li>Using the browser, perform <strong>drag-and-drop</strong> or tab group management to add <strong>Tab D</strong> into the same Tab Group containing Tab A, B, and C.</li>
<li>Observe that:
<ul>
<li>The <strong>omnibox/URL bar</strong> in Tab B, C, and D shows the <strong>URL of their respective tabs</strong>.</li>
<li>Despite this, the <strong>permissions originate from Tab A</strong> and can now be accessed or triggered from Tab B, Tab C, and Tab D, <strong>regardless of their origin</strong>.</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ol>
<h1>Problem Description</h1>
<p>When a tab with active permissions (Tab A) is placed in a Tab Group, other tabs in the same group (Tab B, Tab C) and newly added tabs (Tab D) can access these permissions without user consent.</p>
<p>Although the omnibox/URL bar correctly shows each tab’s URL, the <strong>permission context leaks from Tab A</strong>, allowing sensitive APIs (Camera, Microphone, Geolocation, etc.) to be triggered from unrelated tabs.</p>
<p>Expected behavior: permissions should remain strictly bound to the requesting tab and origin, and must not be accessible by other tabs even within the same Tab Group.</p>

<h3>Additional Note – Extended Impact</h3>
<p>This issue is <strong>not limited to location or permission APIs only</strong>.</p>
<p>Because permissions and modal dialogs are effectively <strong>shared across tabs within the same Tab Group</strong>, this also impacts <strong>JavaScript dialogs such as <code>alert()</code>, permission prompts, and other modal UI</strong>.</p>
<p>An attacker can exploit the <strong>shared Tab Group state</strong> to display trusted-looking dialogs from unrelated origins and <strong>capture user input or credentials in real time</strong>, even though the omnibox shows a different URL.</p>
<p>This <em>security issue</em>* combined with:</p>
<ul>
<li>Shared permissions across tabs</li>
<li>Modal dialog leakage across web contents</li>
<li>User trust in the omnibox origin</li>
</ul>
