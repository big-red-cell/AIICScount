<h3>The problem</h3>
<h4>Please describe the technical details of the vulnerability</h4>
<p>When a user signs into their Google account in Google Chrome on a laptop, saved credentials are stored locally within the Chrome profile. If the user later signs out of the Google account on the same device without deleting the Chrome profile or clearing local browser data, the locally stored passwords remain accessible.
Signing out of Chrome or removing the Google account from another device (such as a mobile phone) does not invalidate or protect the locally stored Chrome profile on the laptop. The Chrome profile continues to exist independently of the Google account’s signed-in state.
As a result, any person with access to the same local operating system user account on the laptop can open Google Chrome and navigate to the Password Manager (chrome://password-manager/passwords). At this point, previously saved website credentials can be viewed after authenticating only with the device’s system password. Re-authentication to the original Google account is not required.
This behavior creates a scenario where signing out of a Google account does not fully revoke access to sensitive credential data stored locally. Users may reasonably expect that signing out of their Google account would prevent further access to saved passwords, but the data remains available as long as the local Chrome profile persists</p>
<h4>Impact analysis</h4>
<p>This may lead to unintended disclosure of sensitive credentials on laptops that are reused, borrowed, lost, or accessed by unintended users, especially when the original user assumes account sign-out fully protects their saved passwords.</p>
